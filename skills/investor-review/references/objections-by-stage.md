# Objections by Stage Reference

The hard questions investors and boards ask at each stage, what they are really testing, what a strong answer contains, and what a weak answer sounds like. Use this to build the "10 hardest questions" list: pick from the reviewed stage, then sharpen each one to the specific business.

---

## How a Strong Answer Is Built

Every strong answer follows the same shape:

1. **Answer first.** One sentence that directly answers the question. No preamble.
2. **One number.** The metric that backs it up, with its time period.
3. **One piece of evidence.** A customer, cohort, contract, test result or document.
4. **The honest gap.** What you don't know yet and how you'll find out.
5. **What you'd do if wrong.** The fallback or the trigger to change course.

**Weak answers share these tells:**
- Starting with "Great question" or with background
- Adjectives instead of numbers ("strong growth", "huge market")
- Defensiveness or attacking the premise
- "We have no competitors" / "No one else is doing this"
- Answering a different, easier question
- Numbers that change between the deck, the data room and the answer

**When you don't know:** "I don't have that number with me. It's [best estimate, labelled as such]; I'll send the exact figure by [day]." Then send it. Bluffing a number that later doesn't reconcile is worse than not knowing.

## Universal Questions (any stage)

| # | Question | What they're testing | A strong answer includes |
|---|----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| U1 | "Why you?" | Founder-market fit | A specific experience, insight or access others don't have; one proof point (customers you already know, a system you built before). |
| U2 | "Who else is doing this, and why do you win?" | Market awareness, honesty | 2–3 named competitors and the incumbent alternative (often spreadsheets or doing nothing); the one dimension where you win, with evidence (win rate, switch story). |
| U3 | "What's the biggest risk to this business?" | Self-awareness | A real risk (not "execution"), how you're testing it, and the early signal that would tell you it's materialising. |
| U4 | "What happens if [big platform / incumbent] builds this?" | Defensibility | Why they won't (counter-positioning, small market for them, conflicts with their model) or what you'll have by then (data, integrations, switching costs). |
| U5 | "What did you believe 6 months ago that turned out wrong?" | Learning speed | A concrete belief, the evidence that changed it, and the decision you made because of it. |
| U6 | "Why does this need to be venture-backed?" | Fit with investor model | The scale potential and why speed matters (winner-take-most market, land-grab, capital-intensive build). |

## Bootstrapped (self-funding check)

| # | Question | What they're testing | A strong answer includes |
|---|----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| B1 | "Can this pay you before it needs outside money?" | Default alive | Months to founder salary at current growth; what revenue that needs; the plan if growth stalls. |
| B2 | "What's the cheapest test of the next step?" | Capital discipline | A test costing under a month of expenses, with a pass/fail threshold set in advance. |
| B3 | "Can customers pay you upfront?" | Cash conversion | Annual plans, deposits or retainers already used or tested; % of revenue collected upfront. |
| B4 | "If I gave you $100K, what would change?" | Whether capital is a crutch | Something specific that money accelerates (not "we'd hire") with expected payback. If nothing changes, don't raise. |
| B5 | "What's your personal runway?" | Founder risk | Months of personal savings or side income; the date by which the business must pay you. |
| B6 | "Why will customers keep paying in a year?" | Retention | Churn so far; the reason customers stay (results, habits, switching costs). |

## Angel / Pre-Seed

The bet is mostly on the team, the insight and the timing.

| # | Question | What they're testing | A strong answer includes |
|---|----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| P1 | "What's the insight others are missing?" | Non-obvious thinking | A specific, slightly contrarian belief backed by something you saw first-hand. |
| P2 | "How do you know people want this?" | Problem validation | Number of customer conversations, how many have the problem acutely, what they do today and what they pay for it; LOIs, pilots or pre-orders. |
| P3 | "Why now?" | Timing | The specific change (technology, regulation, behaviour) and evidence it has happened. |
| P4 | "How big can this get?" | Venture scale | Bottom-up math: number of buyers × price, first wedge, then the expansion market. |
| P5 | "Are you both full-time? What's the vesting?" | Commitment | Yes, with dates; standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff; clean cap table. |
| P6 | "What will this money prove?" | Milestone thinking | 2–3 measurable milestones that make the seed round easy, with the date and the cost. |
| P7 | "What's the first channel to your first 100 customers?" | Go-to-market realism | A named channel you've already tested, with cost and conversion data, even if small. |
| P8 | "Who's the technical founder / who sells?" | Team gaps | The person, or the plan and timeline to fill the gap (and what equity is reserved). |

## Seed

The bet is that early pull becomes repeatable revenue.

| # | Question | What they're testing | A strong answer includes |
|---|----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| S1 | "What's your MRR and month-over-month growth for the last 6 months?" | Traction quality | Exact MRR, monthly growth rates (not an average hiding a spike), and what drove each step. |
| S2 | "What's churn, and why do customers leave?" | Retention | Logo and revenue churn; top 2–3 churn reasons from exit interviews; what you changed. |
| S3 | "How do you acquire customers, and what does it cost?" | Repeatability | Channel mix, CAC by channel including salaries, sales cycle length. |
| S4 | "Is this a product or a feature?" | Defensibility | The job done end-to-end, workflow depth, data that accumulates, expansion modules. |
| S5 | "What are your gross margins?" | Business model | Gross margin with all delivery costs (hosting, model/API costs, support, onboarding). |
| S6 | "What milestones get you to Series A, and does this round fund them?" | Capital plan | ARR target, growth rate, retention target, and the month you'll hit them; runway with 6 months buffer. |
| S7 | "Who's your best customer, and how many more like them exist?" | ICP clarity and market | A named customer profile, why they love it, and the count of similar buyers. |
| S8 | "How much of revenue comes from the top 3 customers?" | Concentration risk | The %; if > 30%, why it's temporary and the pipeline that dilutes it. |
| S9 | "What does a customer pay, and could they pay 2x?" | Pricing power | Current ACV, the value delivered (ROI), willingness-to-pay data, planned price changes. |
| S10 | "Why is the burn what it is?" | Efficiency | Burn breakdown, burn multiple, and which spend is investment vs waste. |

## Series A

The bet is that a repeatable go-to-market machine can be scaled with capital.

| # | Question | What they're testing | A strong answer includes |
|---|----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| A1 | "Show me cohorts. Does net revenue retention exceed 100%?" | Retention and expansion | Cohort table by quarter, NRR and GRR, the expansion driver (seats, usage, upsell). |
| A2 | "Is the sales motion repeatable without the founder?" | Scalability | Non-founder reps closing, quota attainment %, ramp time, win rate by segment. |
| A3 | "What's CAC payback by channel?" | Unit economics | Fully-loaded CAC, gross-margin-adjusted payback in months, by channel and segment. |
| A4 | "What's your burn multiple and how does it trend?" | Capital efficiency | Last 4 quarters' burn multiple and the plan to bring it under 2. |
| A5 | "How do you get to $10M ARR, and in how long?" | Growth plan | Bottom-up plan: reps × quota × attainment, plus PLG or channel contribution, by quarter. |
| A6 | "Who are your next three executive hires?" | Organisational scaling | Roles, why, timing, and what each one unlocks. |
| A7 | "Where do you lose deals, and to whom?" | Competitive honesty | Win/loss data, loss reasons, product or pricing changes made in response. |
| A8 | "What's in the pipeline, and how reliable is it?" | Forecast quality | Stage-weighted pipeline, historical conversion per stage, forecast accuracy last 2 quarters. |
| A9 | "What does the second product or segment look like?" | Expansion path | Evidence of pull (customers asking, early revenue) and why the same team can win it. |
| A10 | "What would make you miss the plan?" | Risk awareness | The 2 assumptions the plan depends on most, current data on each, early-warning metric. |

## Growth (Series B and later)

The bet is efficient scale and a path to a large, durable outcome.

| # | Question | What they're testing | A strong answer includes |
|---|----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| G1 | "What's your Rule of 40 score?" | Balance of growth and profit | Growth % + EBITDA or FCF margin %, trend over 4–8 quarters. |
| G2 | "When are you profitable, and what has to be true?" | Path to profitability | Date, margin assumptions, and the levers (pricing, efficiency, mix) with sensitivity. |
| G3 | "How big is the market you've actually penetrated?" | Market depth | Penetration of core segment, remaining headroom, evidence of adjacent-market traction. |
| G4 | "How durable is your moat as you scale?" | Defensibility at scale | Retention trends in older cohorts, price increases that held, network or data effects measured. |
| G5 | "What's your NRR by segment and how has it moved?" | Expansion quality | NRR by segment, drivers, and what happens in a downturn (seat contraction exposure). |
| G6 | "What's the exit path?" | Return logic | Comparable public companies or acquirers, multiples, scale needed for IPO readiness. |
| G7 | "How do you allocate the next dollar?" | Capital allocation | Return on each use (sales capacity, product, M&A, geography) with payback data. |
| G8 | "What does the board look like, and are controls IPO-ready?" | Governance | Independent directors, audit committee, audited financials, revenue recognition policy. |

## Corporate Board / Investment Committee

The bet is that this is the best use of the group's capital, at acceptable risk to the core business.

| # | Question | What they're testing | A strong answer includes |
|---|----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| C1 | "Why is this a better use of capital than [buyback / core capex / M&A]?" | Capital allocation | Return vs the group's hurdle rate and vs the best alternative use; strategic value beyond the numbers. |
| C2 | "What's the NPV, IRR and payback, and at what discount rate?" | Financial case | NPV at the hurdle rate, IRR, simple and discounted payback; source of every key assumption. |
| C3 | "What happens to the numbers if revenue is 20% lower or a year late?" | Sensitivity | Sensitivity table, the breakeven assumption, and the scenario that makes NPV negative. |
| C4 | "What's the risk to the core business?" | Downside protection | Cannibalisation estimate, brand/channel conflict, regulatory exposure, and mitigations. |
| C5 | "What did we consider instead, including doing nothing?" | Option rigour | 2–3 options compared on the same basis, with the cost of doing nothing quantified. |
| C6 | "How do we stop it if it isn't working?" | Governance | Stage gates with tranche release, kill criteria agreed in advance, review dates. |
| C7 | "Who's accountable, and are they full-time on it?" | Ownership | A named executive owner, dedicated lead, and decision rights. |
| C8 | "Why can't we buy or partner instead of build?" | Build/buy/partner | Comparison of cost, time-to-market, control and integration risk across the three. |
| C9 | "Are the cash flows incremental?" | Analytical honesty | Only revenue and costs that wouldn't happen without the initiative; sunk costs excluded; shared costs allocated fairly. |
| C10 | "What exactly are you asking us to approve today?" | Decision clarity | One resolution: amount, scope, tranches, conditions and the date of the next review. |

## Selecting and Sharpening the 10 Hardest Questions

1. Start with the reviewed stage's table (or the corporate table). Add the universal questions.
2. Rank by where the scorecard is weakest: at least 6 of the 10 must target criteria scored 1–3.
3. Rewrite each question with the company's specifics: name the competitor, the metric, the customer segment, the number. "Who else is doing this?" becomes "The practice-management system your customers already pay for has a scheduling module. Why would they pay you separately?"
4. Order hardest first: the question most likely to end the meeting goes at #1.
5. For each, write the prep note: the specific number to calculate, document to prepare, or test to run before the meeting.
